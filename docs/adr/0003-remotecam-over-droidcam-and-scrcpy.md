# RemoteCam (USB) for phone-as-camera, not DroidCam or scrcpy

The spec originally named DroidCam at "1080p 60 FPS" with "sub-10ms perceived latency." Neither claim holds up: DroidCam's free tier caps at 640x480 (1080p60 needs paid Pro), and its phone-encode -> USB relay -> client-decode pipeline realistically adds tens to 100+ms versus a native UVC webcam even over USB — incompatible with a sub-10ms target for real-time pen tracking.

The best-documented low-latency alternative, `scrcpy --v4l2-sink` (35-70ms glass-to-glass), is Linux-only (`v4l2loopback` has no native Windows equivalent), and the team's dev/demo machine is Windows. WSL2 with USB passthrough was rejected as a bring-up risk the 11-day timeline can't absorb. We chose **RemoteCam** (github.com/Ruddle/RemoteCam, open-source, USB-based) instead: it runs natively on Windows, even though its real-world latency is unbenchmarked and likely worse than scrcpy's. If RemoteCam proves too slow once measured, scrcpy+WSL2 is the documented fallback plan, not a last-minute scramble. All "sub-10ms" language is dropped from pitch materials in favor of "low-latency" plus a measured number once the real pipeline is benchmarked (see spec Section 3.2.C).
